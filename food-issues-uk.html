❓ The problem (problématique)

How equal are the British in their access to quality food?
Les Britanniques sont-ils égaux dans leur accès à une alimentation de qualité ?

Short answer: not equal at all. Millions of people cannot afford healthy food. Poor families either go hungry or eat cheap, unhealthy food — so food insecurity and obesity are two sides of the same coin.

1. Food insecurity (Doc 1)

Definition: food insecurity is when people don't have access to enough healthy, nutritious, quality food — often because proper food has become unaffordable.

In the EU, Sweden is the least affected country, whereas Lithuania is the most affected. The UK does worse than most Western European countries.

2. Consequences on children (Doc 1 — The Conversation, 2020)

  • Holiday hunger: during school holidays, children go without the food normally provided at school (school meals).
  • Education losses: US research showed that after the summer holidays children had lost about one month's worth of skills — and poorer children fare worst.
  • Mental health: anxiety, stress, depression, even suicidal episodes in teenagers.
  • Physical health: chronic illnesses (asthma), and later cancer, diabetes, heart disease and obesity.
  • The government advises a balanced diet (the Eatwell guidelines, Public Health England). Children who don't eat well don't perform as well at school.

Why are more and more people obese? (Cancer Research UK)

  • Obesity has many root causes, and one of the biggest is the world we live in (our environment).
  • When unhealthy options are the easiest, cheapest or only thing available, weight gain becomes more likely.
  • Over time food has become cheaper, larger, tastier and more calorific.
  • Food advertising and marketing have become more sophisticated: millions are spent every year to make us eat unhealthily.

4. The government's answer (The Guardian, Nov 2020)

  • Downing Street unveiled plans to ban online junk food advertising — the toughest digital marketing restrictions in the world — to tackle the obesity crisis.
  • Targeted food: products too high in fat, salt and sugar.
  • Children under 16 were exposed to 15 billion online junk food ads in 2019, compared with 700 million two years earlier.
  • Digital marketing affected: ads on Facebook, paid search on Google, text message promotions, social media on Twitter and Instagram.
  • Matt Hancock (health secretary) wants to help families make healthier choices. Fran Bernhardt (Children's Food Campaign) calls it a world-leading policy: current regulations are inadequate to protect children.

6. Model answer (to learn for the oral)

The British are far from equal in their access to quality food. More than 8 million people are food insecure, which means they cannot afford enough healthy food. The poorer families are, the more likely they are to skip meals or to buy cheap junk food. As a consequence, children suffer from hunger during the holidays, they don't perform as well at school and they are more prone to stress and obesity. Obesity has almost doubled since 1993, due to the world we live in: food is cheaper, larger and more calorific, and we are constantly exposed to ads. That is why the government decided to ban online junk food advertising. In my opinion, this policy is a good start, but the government should also make healthy food more affordable.
